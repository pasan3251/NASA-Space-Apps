# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

School-age space enthusiasts aged 12–16. First release targets desktop computers.

## Product Purpose

An interactive expedition journal introduces NASA hardware and the science it enabled through a page-turning book and three explorable 3D episodes.

## Operating Context

NASA Space Apps demonstration by a five-member team including a 3D artist. Mission content is provisional until the competition briefing on 14 November 2026. Submission is due the evening of 15 November in Asia/Colombo.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Closed book, introduction, contents, mission briefings, first-person surface exploration, overhead inspection, deep-space jetpack EVA, proximity inspection, historical archives, evidence tasks, journal progress, and source links. Curiosity is built first; Surveyor 3/Apollo 12 and Pioneer 10 are provisional companions. The original simulator is preserved in the ignored local `Visual-Testing/` archive; the active application is organized by feature at the repository root. Separate content from shared interaction systems.

## Brand Commitments

User-approved illustrated expedition journal with cel-shaded planetary environments. English-first content, optional narration. Working project name is provisional.

## Evidence on Hand

Existing Curiosity GLB and NASA-Trek-derived terrain require provenance and scale verification. NASA/JPL mission pages and PDS archives supply historical evidence. No verified Surveyor 3 model is present. No future astronaut visit or future hardware state is a historical claim.

## Product Principles

- Hardware purpose, mission history, scientific evidence, and legacy form one story.
- Label documented history, reconstruction, and fictional expedition distinctly.
- All scientific claims and assets retain sources and credits.
- Three compact complete episodes outrank optional simulation complexity.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Keyboard-accessible book and inspection lists, visible focus, reduced motion, readable text, and non-WebGL historical-record fallback. No essential information requires audio or precise first-person movement.
